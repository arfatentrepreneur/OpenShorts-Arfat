# Notice and action: what we actually do when a report arrives

The public half of this lives at `/report-content` and `/reportar-contenido`
(`dashboard/seo/legal.js`). This file is the half nobody outside sees: the
timings, the templates and the rule for repeat infringers. Publishing a
mechanism we do not run is worse than publishing nothing, because the page is
then evidence that we knew what was required.

## What applies to us, and what does not

TONVI TECH SL is a **hosting service** and a **micro enterprise**. That
combination decides most of this, so check it before adding work:

| Duty | Applies? |
|---|---|
| Arts. 11-12, single point of contact (users, authorities) | Yes. `info@openshorts.app`, published |
| Art. 14, terms with the restrictions we impose | Yes. Terms §8 and §9 |
| Art. 16, notice and action | **Yes. This document** |
| Art. 17, statement of reasons | **Yes. Template below** |
| Art. 15, transparency reports | No. Micro/small exemption, art. 15(2) |
| Arts. 20-28, complaints system, trusted flaggers, ODR obligations | No. Those bind *online platforms*, and art. 19 exempts micro/small ones anyway |

We are not an online platform: we store clips at the direction of a user, we do
not disseminate them to the public. Publishing to a user's own social accounts
is done on their instruction and they are the publisher, which Terms §10 says.
If that ever changes (a public gallery, shared clip feeds), this table changes
with it and arts. 20-24 come into play.

## The clock

1. **Acknowledge receipt without undue delay.** Art. 16(4). Same working day.
   This is the step most likely to be missed, because it has to happen before
   anyone has decided anything.
2. **Decide diligently, non-arbitrarily, objectively.** Art. 16(6). Target 72
   hours; sooner for anything involving minors, which goes first, always.
3. **Tell the notifier what we decided and why**, with their redress options.
   Art. 16(5).
4. **Tell the affected user**, with the statement of reasons below. Art. 17.

A notice that carries all four elements the public page asks for gives us
**actual knowledge**. From that moment the hosting exemption of art. 6 stops
covering that specific content, so the clock is not a courtesy.

## Templates

**Acknowledgement** (immediately, to the notifier)

> We received your report on <date> and it has reference <id>. We will review it
> and write back with our decision and the reasons for it. If we need anything
> else to identify the content, we will ask.

**Decision, to the notifier**

> About your report <id> of <date>: we have <removed the content / disabled
> access to it / decided not to act>. <One paragraph of why.> If you disagree,
> you can take the matter to a court, or to an out-of-court dispute settlement
> body certified under Article 21 DSA.

**Statement of reasons, to the affected user** (art. 17(3) wants all of these,
so keep every line even when one is short)

> - What we did: <removed / disabled access / restricted the account>, and to
>   which content, identified precisely.
> - Facts relied on: <what the notice said, what we checked ourselves>.
> - Ground: <the law it breaches, or the clause of our Terms>.
> - Automated means: none. A person reviewed this. <Change only if that stops
>   being true.>
> - Territorial scope: <worldwide / EU only>.
> - How to challenge it: reply to this message, or write to
>   info@openshorts.app. A person reviews it. You may also go to an Article 21
>   dispute settlement body or to the courts.

## Repeat infringers

The public pages say repeat infringers are terminated; here is what that means,
because "reasonably implemented" is the test both the DSA and DMCA 512(i) use
and an unwritten rule fails it.

- One substantiated notice against a user is a **strike**. A notice we rejected
  is not.
- Strike 1 and 2: content removed, user told, strike recorded with the notice
  reference and date.
- **Strike 3: the account is terminated** and re-registration blocked, as Terms
  §8 allows.
- A strike ages out after 12 months.
- Manifest cases (CSAM, a court order, an obvious wholesale rip of a
  commercial film) skip the count and terminate immediately.

Record strikes where they survive a redeploy, not in someone's inbox.

## Bad-faith notifiers

Someone who repeatedly sends manifestly unfounded notices gets a **warning
naming the behaviour**, then suspension of the processing of their notices for
a reasonable period. Warning first, always: art. 23(2) allows the suspension
only after it, and the pattern has to be more than two mistakes.

## Retention

Notices, decisions and strikes are kept 5 years, matching the rights
declarations in the privacy policy (§5). They are the evidence that we acted
diligently, and evidence nobody kept is evidence nobody has.
